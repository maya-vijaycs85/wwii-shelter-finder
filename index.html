    YEAR 6 COMPUTING & HISTORY GUIDE

    How Does the WWII Shelter Finder Work?

    1. Mapping Coordinates (X and Y Grid)

    Just like a modern GPS app uses latitude and longitude, our 1940s London map uses an (x, y) coordinate grid from 0 to 800 across and 0 to 500 down. Every shelter has its own coordinates stored in JavaScript.

    2. Finding the Nearest Shelter (Pythagoras’ Theorem)

    When you click on the map, the computer measures the horizontal distance ($\Delta x$) and vertical distance ($\Delta y$) to every shelter and calculates the straight-line distance using the formula: distance = Math.hypot(shelter.x - user.x, shelter.y - user.y). Then it sorts the list from smallest distance to largest!

    3. Why Would This Have Mattered in 1940?

    During the Blitz (September 1940 – May 1941), German planes bombed London for 57 nights in a row. In the pitch-black Blackout (with streetlights turned off), people visiting unfamiliar streets often didn’t know where the nearest shelter was or if a Tube station was already full. An app with a dimmed Blackout Mode and live capacity numbers could have guided thousands of families to safety in minutes!
